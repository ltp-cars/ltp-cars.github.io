London Airport Transfers: How to Plan the Easiest Ride To and From Every London Airport

Updated October 2026 · Airport travel guide

Getting to or from the airport is the part of a trip most people think about last, yet it is often the part that causes the most stress. That is why LTP Cars put together this guide, drawing on everyday experience of arranging airport journeys for families, groups and business travellers. London has six major airports scattered in every direction around the city, and the right way to reach each one depends on where you are starting, what time your flight is and how much luggage you are carrying.

This guide focuses purely on airport transfers by road. It explains how each London airport differs, what to think about when choosing a vehicle, and which details to sort out before you book so that your journey runs smoothly from the front door to the departures hall, or from arrivals to your hotel.

Why a Pre-Booked Airport Transfer Makes Sense

Public transport works well for a light traveller heading into central London at midday. It becomes much harder at 4am with two suitcases, a pushchair and tired children, or after a long-haul flight when you simply want to sit down and be taken straight to your destination.

A pre-booked london airport transfer removes most of that uncertainty. The vehicle is chosen in advance to suit your group, the fare is agreed before you travel, and for arrivals the driver can follow your flight so the pickup adjusts if you land early or late. Instead of juggling trains, Underground lines and taxi ranks, you take one direct journey.

  • A vehicle sized for your passengers and your luggage
  • A fixed fare agreed before the day of travel, with no meter running in traffic
  • Free flight tracking and 60 minutes free waiting for arrivals
  • Free meet and greet in the arrivals hall
  • TfL-licensed, DBS-checked drivers
  • Availability 24/7 for very early departures and late-night landings

London's Six Airports at a Glance

Heathrow (LHR)

West of the city with multiple terminals. The busiest choice for long-haul flights and families with heavy luggage.

Gatwick (LGW)

South of London, split into North and South Terminals. Always confirm which terminal your airline uses.

Stansted (STN)

North-east of London and popular for European routes, with many very early morning departures.

Luton (LTN)

North of London, used heavily by both holiday and business passengers heading to Europe.

London City (LCY)

Close to Canary Wharf and the City, making it a favourite for business travellers on tight schedules.

Southend (SEN)

East of London. A direct road transfer avoids a long cross-city journey for passengers from the north or west.

Heathrow Airport Transfers

Heathrow handles more passengers than any other UK airport, and its terminals are spread out. When booking, always give the exact terminal number. Journey times from central and east London can change a lot depending on the M4 and M25, so leave a sensible margin for departures, especially at peak times.

Gatwick Airport Transfers

Gatwick's two terminals are linked by a shuttle, but being dropped at the wrong one still costs valuable minutes. Check your booking confirmation for North or South Terminal. A road transfer is particularly useful for groups, as the luggage and passenger count can be planned before the vehicle arrives.

Stansted Airport Transfers

Many Stansted flights leave at dawn, before public transport is fully running. A pre-booked car means you are not relying on the first train of the day or a long night-time connection with luggage in tow.

Luton Airport Transfers

When requesting a Luton quote, give the full pickup postcode, the number of travellers and a realistic description of your bags. These details decide which vehicle is suitable and help avoid problems on the day.

London City Airport Transfers

London City is the closest airport to the financial districts. Even so, business passengers often prefer a direct car so they can travel straight from the aircraft to a meeting, office or hotel without carrying bags through stations.

Southend Airport Transfers

Southend can feel far away if you live in west or north London. A single road transfer turns what might be several connections into one straightforward journey.

Airport-to-Airport Transfers

Connecting between two London airports is one of the most awkward journeys to make by public transport, especially with checked bags and a tight connection. A direct road transfer links the airports without changing trains or crossing central London on the Underground. Popular connections include Heathrow to Gatwick, Heathrow to Stansted, Heathrow to Luton, Gatwick to Stansted and Gatwick to Luton. Because the driver tracks your incoming flight, the pickup is timed to your actual landing rather than the scheduled time.

Choosing the Right Vehicle for Your Airport Transfer

The most common booking mistake is choosing a vehicle by seat count alone. A standard saloon may seat four passengers, but it will not always fit four large suitcases in the boot. Always describe both the number of people and the amount and size of luggage.

  • Saloon: suits one to three passengers with standard luggage.
  • Estate: extra boot space for longer trips or bulky bags.
  • MPV / people carrier: families and small groups with several suitcases.
  • Executive car: business travellers who want extra comfort on the way to or from a flight.
  • 8-seater minibus: larger groups flying out or arriving together.
  • Minibuses and coaches: for bigger parties, with options for groups of up to around 70 passengers.

If you are travelling with young children, ask about child seats when you book rather than on the day, so the correct seats can be fitted in advance.

Your Airport Transfer Booking Checklist

Before you confirm, run through these details. Clear information at the booking stage is the simplest way to make sure the right car turns up at the right place and time.

  • Pickup date and time
  • Full pickup and drop-off addresses with postcodes
  • Airport name and terminal
  • Flight number for any airport pickup
  • Total number of passengers, including children
  • Number and size of suitcases, plus any pushchairs or sports equipment
  • Any extra stops along the way
  • A mobile number the driver can reach on the day

Arriving in London: What Happens After You Land

For airport pickups, giving your flight number is essential. It allows the operator to follow the flight and adjust the collection time if it is delayed or lands early. Most pre-booked services include a period of free waiting after landing, which gives you time to clear passport control and collect your bags. Meet and greet in the arrivals hall means you look for a driver holding your name rather than searching car parks or taxi ranks.

  • 2. Choose your vehicle: based on passengers and luggage, from saloon to minibus.
  • 3. Confirm and receive driver details: your fixed fare is confirmed and driver details are sent before pickup.
